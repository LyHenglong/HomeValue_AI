# ZIP-code feature experiment

Generated by `python src/zip_experiment.py --report` from `reports/zip_experiment_validation.json`. **The production model was not changed**: the SHA-256 hashes of `models/house_price_model.joblib`, `location_support.csv`, `feature_schema.json` and `model_metadata.json` were identical before and after every stage.

## Question

Does adding the ZIP code to the location features improve the recorded-price model, and does it hurt predictions for locations the model has not seen?

## Design

- **Data.** The same cleaned complete-case dataset (696,287 sold-status rows). ZIP codes are stored as 5-character strings (the raw CSV parses them as integers, losing leading zeros; Phase 2 restored them). 40,234 rows have a leading-zero ZIP; 0 are missing or invalid. 18,194 ZIPs in total, 16,451 in the training split, median 7 training rows per ZIP.
- **ZIP as a category, not a number.** `normalize_zip` keeps 5-digit strings, cuts ZIP+4, zero-pads integers (that is exactly how leading zeros were lost) and rejects anything ambiguous (for example the string '601'), `00000`, NaN or booleans; rejected ZIPs become missing.
- **Encoding.** The production encoder (state, then city) gains a third level: the size-adjusted mean log price of the ZIP, shrunk toward its city's value, plus log(1 + training rows in the ZIP). It is learned on training rows only and cross-fitted (5 folds), so no training row's own price enters its own encoding. A missing ZIP or a ZIP not seen in training gets exactly its city's value.
- **Everything else identical to production:** the same split (hash of `property_group`; the split counts are asserted to equal production and no group crosses splits), the same features, gradient-boosting settings and seed. The baseline refit reproduces the production validation MAE ($106,871).
- **Candidates.** (1) the plain ZIP model, with shrinkage strength chosen on validation from [5.0, 20.0, 50.0] (chosen: 5); (2) the same with *ZIP dropout*, hiding the ZIP on 20% of training rows; (3) a *hybrid* that uses the ZIP model only when the ZIP has at least 5 training rows and the production model otherwise.
- **Generalisation checks (validation rows only).** (a) 15% of cities held out of training entirely; (b) 15% of ZIPs held out of training, evaluated on homes in cities that remain in training.
- **Decision rule, fixed before any result was seen:** a candidate must cut validation MAE by at least 2% and must not worsen median % error by more than 1 point on unseen cities or 1 point on unseen ZIPs. Among passing candidates, the lowest validation MAE wins. Only that candidate is evaluated on the test split, once (the script refuses a second test run).

**Finding.** The plain ZIP model is the most accurate on ordinary validation rows, but it is clearly worse for homes in ZIPs it has never seen (median % error +6.7 points). Likely cause (consistent with the dropout result, not proven): in training, 'no ZIP information' only arises for ZIPs with very few rows (when cross-fitting leaves a ZIP empty in a fold), so the model ties that signal to small, atypical ZIPs and misapplies it to new ZIPs in large cities. ZIP dropout repairs most of this (+0.6 points); the hybrid avoids it by construction, because a ZIP without enough training rows goes to the unchanged production model. Unseen *cities* improve slightly with ZIP information, probably because 2,769 ZIPs span several cities and still carry information from a neighbouring city.

## Limitations

- **No prediction intervals yet.** The production 80%/90% ranges were calibrated for the production model. Adopting the hybrid requires refitting the spread model and recalibrating the conformal multipliers on the calibration split, then re-checking coverage; until then, the hybrid has no valid range.
- **The location support table is model-specific.** It was built from the production model's held-out errors; it would need to be rebuilt for the hybrid (cities may move between supported and limited).
- **New input.** The app would need a ZIP field, validated with `normalize_zip` and checked against the chosen city (2,769 ZIPs span several cities). A mistyped but valid ZIP elsewhere in the city would silently change the estimate, so a ZIP-city consistency check is needed.
- **Two models to maintain** (hybrid). ZIP dropout is a single-model alternative that also passed the rule, at a slightly smaller gain.
- **The test split has been used** by the Phase 4 evaluation and once here; repeated feature experiments against it would gradually overfit it.
- **Same data limits as before:** historical (Oct 2021 - May 2022) recorded prices, unverified as sale prices; ZIP improves location detail but not property detail (condition, age, view).

## Recommendation

**Include ZIP code, using the hybrid design**, but only after the follow-up steps below. The gain held on the untouched test split, appeared in every price band and every location status, and did not cost accuracy for unseen ZIPs or unseen cities. Do **not** ship the plain ZIP model: it fails the pre-registered unseen-ZIP check.

Before replacing production (needs approval):

1. Recalibrate the conformal ranges for the hybrid on the calibration split and re-check coverage.
2. Rebuild the location support table from the hybrid's held-out errors.
3. Add an optional, validated ZIP input to `predict.py` and the app, with a ZIP-city consistency check; without a ZIP the app behaves exactly as now.
4. Re-run all tests and the app smoke test.

## Integration into production (model version 2.0.0-zip-hybrid)

Done by `python src/integrate_zip_model.py` after approval. Results: `reports/zip_integration.json`.

### Decisions

- **Two request modes.** *Without a ZIP* the app uses the v1 model and the v1 calibrated ranges, unchanged, so existing behaviour is identical. *With a ZIP* the hybrid is used: the ZIP model when the ZIP has at least 5 training rows, otherwise the v1 model as a city-level fallback (stated to the user).
- **ZIP validation.** ZIPs are 5-character strings (leading zeros kept). A ZIP must appear with the chosen state and city in `models/zip_city_map.csv`, built from the cleaned dataset's (state, city, ZIP) combinations (the same 22,128 combinations as the warehouse location dimension). Malformed or mismatched ZIPs are rejected; no other location is ever substituted. Non-string ZIPs are rejected at the API so leading zeros cannot be lost silently.
- **No retraining.** The experiment's ZIP model was reused; the integration first checks it reproduces the recorded validation MAE ($98,774.59). The encoder code moved to `src/model_common.py` unchanged so production does not depend on the experiment script.
- **Split discipline.** Spread model: validation split. Multipliers: calibration split. Support policy: validation split. Test split: reported below only; nothing computed on it was used for any choice.

### Location support policy v2

Thresholds unchanged from v1 (supported: at least 100 training rows, at least 10 held-out rows, held-out median error at most 25%; limited: at least 30 training rows; otherwise unsupported, no estimate). Changes: the held-out evidence is now the **validation split only** (v1 used validation + calibration), and the error used is the **worse of the no-ZIP and with-ZIP paths**, so a city is supported only if both request modes are reliable. Cities not in the table, and unsupported cities, receive no estimate with or without a ZIP.

### Limitations after integration

- ZIP-path coverage is marginal over test homes that supplied their real ZIP; a user who picks a wrong (but listed) ZIP in the right city gets a confidently wrong ZIP-level estimate. The app labels the ZIP as optional and shows the route used.
- Coverage still drifts for the cheapest and most expensive recorded prices in both modes (see the tables).
- The ZIP-city mapping reflects this dataset only: real ZIPs missing from it are rejected rather than estimated, and the source's own ZIP-city pairs are not independently verified (for example, ZIP 01235, in western Massachusetts, is recorded with Boston).
- The test split has now been looked at three times (Phase 4, ZIP experiment, this check); it was not used for choices here, but future work should rely on validation data or a new hold-out.
- No real-browser or mobile visual check of the app was performed.

### Rollback

1. Stop the app.
2. Copy the four files in `models/backups/v1_no_zip/` back into `models/` (`house_price_model.joblib`, `location_support.csv`, `feature_schema.json`, `model_metadata.json`); check them against `MANIFEST.json`.
3. Delete or move `models/zip_model.joblib` and `models/zip_city_map.csv`. `src/predict.py` then disables ZIP input automatically and behaves exactly like v1.
4. Run `python -m pytest tests -q` and start the app.
